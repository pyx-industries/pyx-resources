## How to Identify Communities That May Be Ready for Traceability, Transparency and Trusted Data Exchange Initiatives

Industry sectors, member organisations and value chains are facing pressure around traceability, transparency and trusted data exchange. While any one of these pressures may not be significant on its own, patterns begin to emerge when multiple organisations experience similar challenges at the same time. How they respond can provide clues about whether a coordinated response may be needed.

[Trust Architects](./TA-rec49.mdx#what-is-the-role-of-a-trust-architect) should therefore look not only for evidence of a problem, but for signs that a community may be becoming ready to organise around a shared approach.

This guide outlines signals that a Trust Architecture opportunity may exist.

## Market Pressure

Procurement and market-access pressures are occurring across many sectors. Watch for associated indicators that suggest change is becoming necessary.

Indicators include:

- Procurement requirements driving new information requests
- New market access requirements tied to transparency, due diligence or compliance obligations
- Customers repeatedly asking for the same information in different formats
- Suppliers struggling to meet overlapping reporting requirements
- Growing requests for supply chain data
- Increasing reporting complexity and compliance burden
- Industry associations highlighting member concerns or capability gaps
- Organisations publicly discussing the cost or difficulty of meeting new requirements
- Repeated calls for standardisation, interoperability or shared approaches
- Working groups forming to address common reporting or traceability challenges

These signals may indicate that existing approaches are becoming outdated, inefficient, costly or difficult to scale.

Communities experiencing these pressures may begin discussing the need for greater coordination, shared solutions or more efficient ways to exchange, verify and manage information. One of the clearest signals is when industry associations begin communicating that their members are struggling with such a challenge but don't know where to start.

## Operational Friction

Not all Trust Architecture opportunities will begin with regulation or procurement requirements. Some will begin with communities developing increasingly complex or siloed workarounds to solve the same problem.

Trust Architects who are already working within a community, industry association or value chain will be well placed to recognise these patterns before they become visible externally.

Watch for:

- Information being recreated multiple times
- The same data being submitted in different formats
- Spreadsheet- or email-based information exchange
- Manual verification processes
- Duplicate audits or assessments
- Organisations describing administrative burden rather than strategic problems
- Different organisations independently describing the same operational frustrations
- Members asking whether others are experiencing the same challenges

These signals may indicate that existing ways of working are becoming increasingly inefficient, costly and difficult to scale.

Communities experiencing these challenges may begin looking for more coordinated ways to exchange, verify and manage information, even if they are not yet describing the problem as one of interoperability or Trust Architecture.

## Industry Coordination

Trust Architecture opportunities may emerge when industries begin organising around shared challenges.

Watch for:

- Industry groups releasing new standards, guidance or frameworks
- Industry guidelines that imply or identify a need for interoperability
- Industry bodies highlighting transparency, sustainability or digital transformation priorities
- Working groups forming around traceability, sustainability reporting or supply chain visibility
- Cross-industry working groups beginning to collaborate
- Industry associations communicating that members are struggling and do not know where to start

These may be signs that multiple organisations are facing the same challenge and that collective action may be possible.

## Trust Signals

Trust signals emerge when organisations need greater confidence in the information being exchanged.

Watch for:

- Growing demand for trusted or verified information
- Questions about provenance claims
- Questions about certifications and compliance claims
- Concerns about fragmentation across platforms, systems or reporting schemes
- Organisations saying things like "everyone is solving this differently"
- Growing pressure to provide evidence rather than simply make claims

These signals may point to the same underlying challenge: communities need better ways to exchange trusted information.

## Where to Look

Useful indicators can emerge from many places, including procurement documents, requests for proposals (RFPs), industry newsletters, conference presentations, webinars, working group discussions, member surveys, media coverage, blog posts, social media conversations and public statements from industry bodies.

Trust Architects should also monitor emerging policy consultations, standards development activities and funding announcements, as these can indicate that broader ecosystem change is underway.

## Looking for Convergence

A single signal does not necessarily indicate a Trust Architecture opportunity.

A new regulation on its own may not be enough.

An industry working group on its own may not be enough.

Questions about provenance on their own may not be enough.

But when multiple signals begin appearing at the same time, communities may become more willing to explore coordinated approaches.

For example:

- Market pressure is increasing
- Industry groups are discussing the issue
- Organisations are struggling with reporting complexity
- Demand for trusted information is growing

:::tip
Trust Architecture opportunities rarely emerge because organisations decide they want interoperability.

More often, they emerge because existing approaches are no longer working.

The signals above can help Trust Architects recognise when a community may be approaching that point.
:::

## What Happens Next?

Trust Architects help communities move from recognising a shared challenge to organising around a practical solution.

The work extends well beyond identifying an opportunity. Communities need support to organise, develop a coordinated approach and ultimately achieve adoption.

When multiple signals begin to converge, the next step is to evaluate whether the opportunity warrants further investigation.

The [**Trust Architecture Opportunity Assessment Template**](https://forum.community.pyx.io/t/trust-architecture-opportunity-assessment-template/78) provides a practical framework for documenting evidence, evaluating emerging opportunities and determining appropriate next steps.

If the assessment indicates that a coordinated approach may be appropriate, the first Community Activation step is **Inception**.

The goal of the Inception phase is to understand the problem, identify the stakeholders involved, explore existing incentives and determine whether a coordinated Community Activation effort should proceed. See [Questions to Ask During the Community Activation Inception Phase](./TA-cap-inception-questions.md).
